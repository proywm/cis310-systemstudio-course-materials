# Final Project: 8-bit Processor and Assembly-Program Demonstration

The culminating CIS 310 project asks students to integrate the course sequence into an **8-bit processor implementation**, an **assembly program**, and a presentation with live or recorded evidence as specified in Canvas.

## Schedule

The presentation and demonstration will occur during **Fall 2026 final examination week**. The exact date, time, room, presentation order, and submission deadline are **to be announced in Canvas**.

## Learning progression

Use these formative checkpoints to plan and self-evaluate. They do not replace the released Canvas requirements or instructor/GSI evaluation.

1. **Architecture and instruction behavior:** Explain the intended 8-bit datapath, instruction representation, registers, memory interaction, and control sequence.
2. **Reusable components:** Verify the register, register-file, ALU, memory, program-counter, and instruction-register behavior before integration.
3. **Datapath and control:** Connect the components and explain how control signals move data through fetch, decode, and execute behavior.
4. **Assembly program:** Prepare and test the assembly program required by the released specification. Be able to explain each instruction and its expected processor-state change.
5. **System evidence:** Demonstrate representative instructions and compare expected with observed register, memory, flag, output, and control behavior.
6. **Presentation:** Rehearse a concise explanation of design choices, test evidence, limitations, and each team member's contribution.

## Planning artifacts

Prepare these likely evidence types while checking Canvas for the authoritative list:

- test cases or a documented test table with expected and observed behavior;
- an architecture or datapath explanation;
- presentation material and demonstration evidence; and
- an individual contribution/explanation record when working in an authorized team.
